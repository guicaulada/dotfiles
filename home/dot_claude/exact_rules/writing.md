# Writing

Applies to all prose: docs, READMEs, PR descriptions, issues, commit bodies, and comments.

## Rules

1. Never use a long word where a short one will do: "use" not "utilize", "helps" not "facilitates".
2. If it is possible to cut a word out, cut it: "to" not "in order to", "now" not "at this point in time".
3. Use the active voice: "the hook blocks the command", not "the command is blocked by the hook".
4. Prefer concrete over abstract — a short example beats a paragraph of description.
5. Never use jargon or buzzwords where plain language works: "improve" not "leverage synergies".
6. Break any of these rules sooner than write something unclear.

## Structure

- Lead with the point. State the conclusion first, then the supporting detail.
- One idea per sentence, one topic per paragraph.
- No hype: skip "powerful", "seamless", "robust", "comprehensive". Say what it does.

## Avoid AI tells

- No significance inflation: don't call things "crucial", "essential", or "a testament to" anything.
- No formulaic structure: skip "It's not just X, it's Y", rule-of-three lists, and generic wrap-up conclusions ("In summary...").
- Don't cycle synonyms to avoid repeating a word — repetition is fine, drift is not.
- No decorative boldface, emoji, or em-dash chains; use plain sentences.
- Don't hedge reflexively ("it could be argued", "may potentially") — commit to a claim or omit it.
